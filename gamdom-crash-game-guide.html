How a round actually works

Each round starts at a 1.00x multiplier and climbs on a curve until it hits a "crash point," a multiplier value fixed for that round before anyone places a bet. Players wager during a short betting window, then watch the multiplier rise and choose when to cash out. If you cash out before the crash point, you win your bet times whatever multiplier was showing the instant you cashed out. If the round crashes before you cash out, you lose the wager. There is no way to see the crash point in advance; the entire game is a race between your nerve and a number that was already set before the round began.

Manual vs. auto cash-out

Manual cash-out means clicking the cash-out button yourself while the multiplier is climbing, which depends entirely on your reaction time and connection latency. Auto cash-out lets you set a target multiplier ahead of time (for example, 2.00x), and the game cashes you out automatically the instant that value is reached, removing reaction time from the equation entirely. Auto cash-out doesn't change your odds; it just removes the risk of missing your intended exit due to lag or hesitation.

How the crash point is generated

Crash point generation on provably fair systems like Gamdom's follows the same seed-and-hash pattern used across its Originals. Before the round opens, the game commits to a server seed and shows you its hash, a scrambled fingerprint that can't be reverse-engineered but can be checked once the real seed is revealed. The final crash point is calculated from that server seed combined with a client seed (which you can set yourself) and a nonce that increments each round. Because the hash is published before you bet, the operator cannot change the outcome after seeing your wager. Why "crash strategies" don't change the math

Cash-out strategies (fixed-multiplier targets, martingale-style bet doubling after a loss, chasing a specific pattern in recent rounds) are common in Crash communities, but none of them change the underlying math: each round's crash point is independently generated, so past rounds have no bearing on the next one. A strategy can change how quickly you win or lose, and how large individual swings feel, but it cannot turn a negative-expectation game into a positive-expectation one over the long run. Treat any claimed "guaranteed" Crash strategy with the same skepticism you'd apply to a guaranteed roulette system, because the underlying math is the same category of problem.

Bankroll basics specific to Crash

Because rounds are fast and the multiplier is visually exciting while it climbs, Crash is one of the easier games to lose track of session length and total wagered amount in. Setting a fixed session budget before you start, and treating auto cash-out targets as a way to remove in-the-moment decisions rather than a way to "beat" the game, are the two most practical habits players describe using. If you notice yourself increasing bet sizes specifically to recover a prior loss, that's the martingale pattern referenced above, and it's worth stepping away rather than continuing.

Crash games built on this model are typically uncapped in principle, though house-edge math makes very high multipliers extremely rare. Check Gamdom's current game rules page for any stated cap, since specifics can change.
