Provably Fair, Explained: How to Verify a Round Wasn't Rigged

You don't have to trust a crypto casino's word that a Crash multiplier or dice roll was fair: you can check the math yourself. Here's how.

The basic idea

A provably fair system commits to a game's outcome before you place your bet, in a way that's mathematically impossible to change afterward, and then reveals the proof so you can verify it after the fact. It works through a combination of a "server seed" (generated by the casino, kept hidden until after the round), a "client seed" (which you can set yourself), and often a nonce (a counter that increments each bet).

Step by step

Before the round, the casino generates a server seed and shows you its hash (a scrambled fingerprint of that seed that can't be reverse-engineered but can be checked once the real seed is revealed). You place your bet, optionally after setting your own client seed. The round resolves using a combination of both seeds. Afterward, the casino reveals the original server seed. You (or a third-party verifier tool) can hash that revealed seed yourself and confirm it matches the hash you were shown before the round, which proves the casino didn't change the outcome after seeing your bet.

How to actually check it

Most provably fair casinos, Gamdom included, build a verifier directly into the game page or account settings, letting you paste in a round's revealed seed and confirm the hash matches what was shown beforehand. The exact location of this tool varies by platform: check your account's "provably fair" or "fairness" settings, or the casino's help center, for the current walkthrough. If you'd rather not rely on the casino's own verifier, independent third-party provably-fair checker tools exist that run the same hash comparison outside the platform entirely.

Why casinos bother with this at all

Provably fair systems exist because crypto-casino players are disproportionately skeptical of centralized "trust us" claims; it's the same audience that generally prefers verifiable systems over institutional trust. Offering a mathematically checkable fairness guarantee is a genuine differentiator for attracting that audience, not just a marketing label.

What this does and doesn't protect you from

Provably fair proves the casino didn't alter a specific round's outcome after your bet. It does not change the house edge, and it doesn't make gambling a winning long-term strategy. It's a fairness guarantee, not a profitability one.

Frequently asked questions

Can a casino cheat a provably fair game?

Not without it being detectable. Because the server seed's hash is shown to you before the round and the seed itself is revealed after, changing the seed after seeing your bet would produce a hash that doesn't match what was shown beforehand, which you or a verifier tool can check.

Does provably fair mean I'll win more?

No. Provably fair verifies that a specific round's outcome wasn't manipulated after your bet; it says nothing about the house edge, which still applies exactly as published regardless of the fairness proof.

Do I need to understand cryptography to verify a round?

No. Most platforms provide a built-in verifier tool that runs the hash check for you: you paste in the revealed seed and it confirms whether it matches. Third-party independent verifier tools exist too if you don't trust the casino's own tool.
